# Regular Cube Draft Guide

## What to Expect

Regular Cube plays like a slightly powered-up Masters set. It's creature-focused, combat matters, and the mana curve is low. Most cards have a reasonable floor even without synergy, but the best decks combine multiple overlapping themes to squeeze extra value out of every card.

The power level is closer to uncommon than rare. Don't expect to slam haymakers -- expect tight games where sequencing, combat tricks, and card interactions decide things. Spells that cost 5 or more mana need to earn their spot. The environment is fast, and your opponents will be putting pressure on you starting turn one or two.

Most successful decks are two colors, sometimes with a light splash. Drafting a straightforward limited plan -- prioritizing removal, efficient creatures, and a good curve -- in any two colors will give you a functional deck. But the best decks layer multiple synergies on top of that foundation.

## How to Draft This Cube

**Synergy over raw power.** There's no single "best card" to first-pick every time. Instead, look for cards that do double or triple duty across themes. A card like Weaponcraft Enthusiast looks simple, but it feeds sacrifice strategies, produces artifact tokens, triggers ETB effects, and puts +1/+1 counter synergies to work. These "bridge cards" are the backbone of strong decks.

**Stay open early.** Themes overlap heavily in this cube, so early picks rarely lock you into one lane. A card that's good in a sacrifice deck might also be great in a tokens deck or a counters deck. Let the signals from what's being passed guide you toward your specific combination.

**Respect the curve.** This is a fast environment. You want to be doing something meaningful on turns one through three. Don't get caught with a hand full of four-drops while your opponent is already attacking.

**Mana fixing is asymmetric.** The more aggressive color pairs (Red-White-Green combinations) get faster, more efficient fixing. Slower color pairs have access to fixing too, but it often enters tapped or comes with a tempo cost. Plan accordingly -- if you're in Blue-Black, you can afford to be a bit slower, but if you're in Red-White, you need your mana working from the start.

## Building Your Deck: Mana Curve

In limited formats like cube draft, you build a 40-card deck. Of those, roughly 17 will be lands and 23 will be spells (creatures, instants, sorceries, etc.). How you distribute those 23 spells across mana costs -- your "mana curve" -- is one of the most important parts of deck building.

Regular Cube is a fast environment, so your curve should lean low. Here's a recommended starting point:

| Mana Cost | Number of Cards | Role |
|-----------|----------------|------|
| 1 mana | 2-4 | Early plays, interaction, setup |
| 2 mana | 5-7 | The backbone of your deck |
| 3 mana | 4-6 | Your most impactful spells |
| 4 mana | 2-4 | Strong plays, but don't overload |
| 5+ mana | 0-2 | Only if the card is game-changing |
| **Lands** | **16-17** | |

**A few notes on this curve:**

- **Two-drops are king.** Having something to play on turn two is critical in this format. If you end up with only three or four two-mana cards, you'll frequently fall behind. Prioritize two-drops during the draft, especially efficient creatures.
- **Aggressive decks** should push the curve even lower -- aiming for 4+ one-drops, 6-7 two-drops, and very few cards above three mana. Aggro decks can sometimes get away with 16 lands.
- **Slower decks** (like Blue-Black value decks) can afford a slightly higher curve, but even then, you need cheap interaction to survive the early turns. Don't mistake "slower" for "do nothing until turn four."
- **Count mana costs, not just cards.** A card with cycling or an alternative cheap mode (like the front half of a split card) can effectively count as a lower-cost spell even if its printed cost is higher.
- **Mana-producing creatures count as both.** A two-mana creature that produces mana (like Voyaging Satyr) fills your two-drop slot while also letting you cast your expensive spells sooner.

## Themes

This cube doesn't have ten rigid archetypes mapped to color pairs. Instead, it has a web of overlapping synergistic themes that span multiple colors. Most decks end up combining two or three of these. The key is finding the intersections.

### Tokens

Tokens are creature cards created by other cards during the game rather than drawn from your deck. The tokens theme is the largest in the cube, spanning every color. White and Black have the deepest token support, with Green and Red contributing meaningfully.

**What to look for:** Cards that create multiple bodies (Weaponcraft Enthusiast, Mogg War Marshal, Resolute Reinforcements), token payoffs (Judith, the Scourge Diva; Corpse Knight; Cruel Celebrant), and anthem effects. Tokens feed naturally into sacrifice strategies and go-wide plans.

**Key bridge cards:** Sling-Gang Lieutenant (tokens + sacrifice + ETB), Saheeli, Sublime Artificer (tokens + spells matter + artifacts), Marionette Master (tokens + counters + artifacts + ETB)

### +1/+1 Counters

Some cards place +1/+1 counter markers on creatures to permanently increase their power and toughness. This is a deep theme centered in Green and White, with support across all colors. Many creatures enter with or accumulate counters naturally.

**What to look for:** Creatures that grow over time (Experiment One, Pelt Collector, Carrion Feeder), counter distributors (Winding Constrictor, Grumgully, the Generous; Pollenbright Druid), and payoffs for having counters (Proliferate effects, Modular). Counters overlap heavily with heroic and ETB themes.

**Key bridge cards:** Battlewise Hoplite (counters + heroic), Scurry Oak (counters + tokens), Emergent Sequence (counters + ramp)

### Sacrifice

Sacrifice strategies revolve around deliberately sending your own creatures to the graveyard for a benefit -- drawing cards, dealing damage, or triggering other effects. Black is the anchor color, with Red as a strong secondary. This theme rewards having expendable creatures and ways to profit from them dying.

**What to look for:** Sacrifice outlets (Viscera Seer, Carrion Feeder, Priest of Forgotten Gods), fodder generators (Nested Shambler, Mogg War Marshal, Weaponcraft Enthusiast), and death payoffs. Sacrifice pairs naturally with tokens for fodder and graveyard themes for recursion.

**Key bridge cards:** Priest of Forgotten Gods (sacrifice + removal + ramp), Blood for Bones (sacrifice + graveyard), Goblin Engineer (sacrifice + artifacts + ETB + graveyard)

### Enter the Battlefield (ETB)

Many creatures have abilities that trigger when they enter the battlefield -- for example, drawing a card, destroying something, or creating a token. This theme is about maximizing those triggers by replaying, blinking, or bouncing those creatures. Blue and White have the most ETB creatures, but this theme touches every color.

**What to look for:** Creatures with strong ETB effects (Mulldrifter, Skinrender, Reflector Mage, Man-o'-War), ways to re-trigger them (Whitemane Lion, Kor Skyfisher, Ephemerate), and value creatures that are worth the extra effort to replay.

**Key bridge cards:** Fiend Hunter (ETB + removal), Thraben Inspector (ETB + tokens), Dungeon Geists (ETB + removal + evasion)

### Spells Matter

This theme rewards you for casting instants, sorceries, and other noncreature spells. Each spell you cast triggers bonus effects on your creatures -- extra damage, extra tokens, or growing your threats. Concentrated in Blue and Red with the Blue-Red gold cards leading the way.

**What to look for:** Prowess and similar triggers (Monastery Swiftspear, Sprite Dragon, Balmor, Battlemage Captain), spell payoffs (Young Pyromancer, Murmuring Mystic, Guttersnipe), and cheap interaction that doubles as fuel. This theme is naturally tempo-oriented.

**Key bridge cards:** Young Pyromancer (spells matter + tokens), Dreadhorde Arcanist (spells matter + graveyard), Saheeli, Sublime Artificer (spells matter + tokens + artifacts)

### Heroic / Targeting

Heroic is a keyword ability that triggers whenever you cast a spell that targets the creature with heroic -- often placing +1/+1 counters or giving other bonuses. This is a small but potent theme primarily in White and Red, with Green contributing pump spells. Only a handful of cards have the actual Heroic keyword, but the enablers do a lot of work on their own.

**What to look for:** Heroic creatures (Favored Hoplite, Fabled Hero, Phalanx Leader), cheap targeting spells that are good on their own (Valorous Stance, Integrity // Intervention, Reckless Rage), and combat tricks. Even divided damage spells like Arc Lightning can trigger heroic while removing a blocker.

**Key bridge cards:** Collision // Colossus (removal + heroic), Lash of Malice (removal + heroic), Flowstone Infusion (removal + heroic)

### Graveyard

The graveyard is where cards go when they're destroyed, discarded, or used up. Some cards can be played again from the graveyard (via flashback, escape, or unearth), and others let you return cards from your graveyard to your hand or the battlefield. Black has the deepest graveyard support, with Red and Green as secondary colors.

**What to look for:** Self-mill and discard effects, cards with flashback or escape (Faithless Looting, Lava Dart, Call of the Herd), recursion (Graveshifter, Blood for Bones, Unearth), and creatures that are happy in the graveyard. Pairs well with sacrifice and cycling themes.

**Key bridge cards:** Lava Dart (removal + sacrifice + graveyard), Call of the Herd (tokens + graveyard), Archaeomender (ETB + artifacts + graveyard)

### Cycling / Discard

Cycling is an ability that lets you pay a cost to discard a card and draw a new one, smoothing out your draws. More broadly, this theme includes "looter" effects that let you draw and discard to filter through your deck. Blue and Black are the primary colors, with Red contributing. This theme gives you card selection and fuels graveyard strategies.

**What to look for:** Looters (Merfolk Looter, Looter il-Kor, Moon-Circuit Hacker), cycling cards (Censor, Neutralize), discard payoffs (Archfiend of Ifnir), and cards that benefit from cards hitting the graveyard. This theme bridges into graveyard and spells matter strategies.

**Key bridge cards:** Archfiend of Ifnir (cycling + removal + counters + evasion), Bone Shards (cycling + removal + sacrifice), Scrapwork Mutt (cycling + ETB + graveyard)

### Evasion

Evasion refers to abilities that make a creature difficult or impossible to block -- flying, shadow, menace, and "can't be blocked" effects. This is one of the broadest themes, spanning every color but concentrated in Blue and White.

**What to look for:** Flyers, shadow, menace, and unblockable creatures. These are less of a "build around" and more of a draft fundamental -- every deck needs ways to push damage through, and evasion creatures are how you do it.

### Aggro

Aggro (short for aggressive) strategies aim to win quickly by deploying cheap, efficient creatures and attacking every turn before your opponent can set up their game plan. Red is the primary aggro color with haste creatures leading the charge. Aggressive strategies in this cube need synergy to reach their full potential -- you won't have a critical mass of hyper-efficient one-drops like a Legacy cube.

**What to look for:** Haste creatures (Monastery Swiftspear, Goblin Guide analog cards, Ahn-Crop Crasher), reach effects, and fast mana fixing. Two-color aggro is viable and even encouraged in this cube.

### Ramp

Ramp refers to effects that accelerate your mana production, letting you cast more expensive spells earlier than your opponent. Green is the home for ramp, with a handful of colorless mana rocks. Ramp in this cube doesn't lead to massive payoffs -- it's more about getting ahead on tempo and casting your three- and four-drops a turn early.

**What to look for:** Mana creatures (Hope Tender, Voyaging Satyr), land-based ramp (Emergent Sequence, Ranging Raptors), and mana rocks (Chromatic Sphere, Prophetic Prism). Note that Burning-Tree Emissary is both ramp and an aggressive play.

### Artifacts Matter

Artifacts are colorless permanent cards -- equipment, vehicles, and artifact creatures. Some cards reward you for controlling or casting artifacts by drawing cards, creating tokens, or growing stronger. This is a small but high-impact theme. There aren't enough artifacts-matter cards to build an entire deck around, but a few key payoffs can dramatically change your draft priorities.

**What to look for:** Artifact payoffs (Jhoira, Weatherlight Captain; Sai, Master Thopterist; Saheeli, Sublime Artificer), artifact recursion (Myr Retriever, Scrap Trawler, Goblin Engineer), and the many artifact creatures and equipment already in your deck. Even a few artifacts-matter cards can turn your Prophetic Prism and equipment into engine pieces.

### Equipment

Equipment are artifact cards that attach to your creatures to give them bonuses. Unlike auras (enchantments that attach to creatures), equipment sticks around when the creature dies and can be moved to another creature. This is a small supporting theme in White and Red. Equipment provides reusable value and pairs with tokens (equip to the best token) and heroic-adjacent strategies.

**Key cards:** Ancestral Blade, Barbed Spike, Mortarpod, Mask of Memory, Rabbit Battery, Simian Sling

## Color Pairs

While there aren't rigid archetypes, each color pair has natural synergy directions signposted by its gold cards. Here's what to look for in each.

### Boros (Red/White) -- Heroic Aggro

The most aggressive color pair, with the fastest mana fixing. Gold cards like Anax and Cymede and Swiftblade Vindicator reward targeting your own creatures, while Heroic Reinforcements and Baird, Argivian Recruiter support a go-wide tokens plan. Figure of Destiny is a flexible threat at any stage.

**Natural themes:** Heroic, tokens, counters, aggro, equipment

### Gruul (Red/Green) -- Counters Beatdown

Gruul hits hard with creatures that grow. Grumgully, the Generous makes every creature enter bigger, Bloodbraid Elf provides card advantage and tempo, and Burning-Tree Emissary enables explosive starts. Fires of Yavimaya gives everything haste. The removal suite (Collision // Colossus, Thrash // Threat, Domri's Ambush) doubles as combat tricks.

**Natural themes:** Counters, aggro, heroic, ramp, tokens

### Selesnya (Green/White) -- Tokens and Counters

Selesnya goes wide and grows tall. Emmara, Soul of the Accord and Voice of Resurgence make tokens, Maja, Bretagard Protector rewards ramping, and Knight of the Reliquary ties together lands and sacrifice. Captured by Lagacs and Prepare // Fight keep your creatures fighting above their weight class.

**Natural themes:** Tokens, counters, ETB, heroic, ramp

### Rakdos (Black/Red) -- Sacrifice Aggro

Rakdos combines aggressive pressure with sacrifice synergies. Judith, the Scourge Diva and Dreadhorde Butcher reward you for attacking and having creatures die. Blazing Specter provides evasive disruption, and Terminal Agony is efficient removal. Angrath, Captain of Chaos ties together tokens and counters.

**Natural themes:** Sacrifice, aggro, tokens, counters, graveyard

### Orzhov (Black/White) -- Sacrifice and Tokens

Orzhov is the premier death-triggers pair. Corpse Knight and Cruel Celebrant drain your opponent whenever creatures enter or leave play. Fleshtaker provides a sacrifice outlet with lifegain, and Tidehollow Sculler disrupts your opponent while being a body worth sacrificing. Graceful Restoration brings things back.

**Natural themes:** Sacrifice, tokens, ETB, counters, graveyard

### Golgari (Black/Green) -- Sacrifice and Counters

Golgari bridges sacrifice and counters themes. Winding Constrictor doubles your counter production, Ravenous Squirrel grows as things die, and Korozda Guildmage turns creatures into tokens. Poison-Tip Archer punishes board wipes, and Slimefoot, the Stowaway generates both tokens and a drain engine.

**Natural themes:** Sacrifice, counters, tokens, graveyard, ramp

### Izzet (Blue/Red) -- Spells Matter

The natural home for the spells-matter theme. Balmor, Battlemage Captain and Sprite Dragon reward cheap interaction, Saheeli, Sublime Artificer turns every noncreature spell into an artifact token, and Jhoira, Weatherlight Captain draws cards off artifacts and historic spells. Tempest Angler and Electrolyze round out the value.

**Natural themes:** Spells matter, artifacts, counters, tokens, aggro

### Dimir (Blue/Black) -- ETB Value and Evasion

Dimir plays a longer game built on card advantage through ETB creatures and evasive threats. Baleful Strix and Ingenious Infiltrator generate cards while attacking, Sphinx Summoner tutors for artifact creatures, and Soul Diviner turns counters into cards. Discovery // Dispersal offers card selection early and removal late.

**Natural themes:** ETB, evasion, cycling, graveyard, sacrifice

### Simic (Blue/Green) -- Counters and Value

Simic grows creatures and generates value. Eutropia the Twice-Favored and Merfolk Skydiver add counters with evasion, Ivy, Gleeful Spellthief copies your targeting spells, and Shardless Agent cascades into value. Scuttling Sentinel provides a large body with counter synergies. River Hoopoe offers a mana sink for long games.

**Natural themes:** Counters, ETB, evasion, tokens, spells matter

### Azorius (Blue/White) -- ETB and Heroic

Azorius combines ETB value with heroic-adjacent strategies. Battlewise Hoplite and Noyan Dar, Roil Shaper reward targeting, Reflector Mage provides tempo, and Chrome Courier and Raff Capashen, Ship's Mage tie into artifact themes. Depose // Deploy and Warrant // Warden are flexible split cards.

**Natural themes:** ETB, heroic, counters, artifacts, evasion

## Draft Tips

1. **Count your two-drops.** This cube is fast. Having 5-7 cards you're happy playing on turn two is a good baseline.

2. **Removal is premium.** Because the power level is flattened, you can't rely on raw card quality to catch up. Efficient removal keeps you in games. Be judicious with it -- sometimes the trick is knowing when *not* to use your removal.

3. **Bridge cards are your best picks.** Cards that touch multiple themes keep your options open during the draft and give your final deck more internal synergy.

4. **Don't force a single theme.** Most themes aren't deep enough to fill an entire deck. The best decks combine two or three themes that share cards. A sacrifice-tokens deck, a counters-heroic deck, or a spells-graveyard deck will outperform a deck trying to be pure sacrifice.

5. **Respect combat tricks.** At this power level, combat tricks are real cards. Your opponent's Blossoming Defense or Integrity // Intervention can blow out a combat step. Attack and block with this in mind.

6. **Splashing is fine, but don't get greedy.** Two colors is the sweet spot. A light splash for a powerful gold card is reasonable, especially if you have fixing. Three full colors is risky in a fast environment.

7. **Lands do things.** The bounce lands (Azorius Chancery, etc.), creature lands (Celestial Colonnade, Creeping Tar Pit), and utility lands add real strategic depth. Don't dismiss them during the draft.

8. **Five-drops need to be great.** If a card costs five or more mana, it needs to have a major impact when it resolves. Most of your deck should cost three or less.

## Glossary

New to Magic or drafting? Here are some common terms used in this guide.

**Aggro:** Short for "aggressive." A strategy that aims to win quickly with cheap, efficient creatures before the opponent can establish their game plan.

**Blinking:** Exiling a creature you control and immediately returning it to the battlefield. This re-triggers any "enters the battlefield" abilities. Also called flickering.

**Bouncing:** Returning a permanent (usually a creature) from the battlefield to its owner's hand. This can be used on your own creatures to re-trigger ETB abilities, or on your opponent's creatures as a form of temporary removal.

**Curve (mana curve):** The distribution of mana costs across your deck. A good curve means you have plays available at every stage of the game -- cheap spells for the early turns and more powerful ones for later. "Curving out" means playing a spell on each consecutive turn (a two-cost spell on turn two, a three-cost spell on turn three, etc.).

**Drops (two-drop, three-drop, etc.):** Shorthand for a card's mana cost. A "two-drop" is a card that costs two mana. "Count your two-drops" means making sure you have enough cheap cards to play in the early game.

**Evasion:** Any ability that makes a creature difficult or impossible to block, such as flying, menace, shadow, or "can't be blocked." Evasion creatures are important for pushing damage through when the board is clogged with creatures on both sides.

**Mana fixing:** Cards that help you produce colors of mana beyond your basic lands. This includes dual lands (lands that produce two colors), mana rocks (artifacts that produce mana), and effects that search your library for specific lands. Good fixing lets you reliably cast spells in a two-color or splash deck.

**Ramp:** Effects that increase your mana production beyond the normal one-land-per-turn rate. Mana-producing creatures, artifacts, and spells that put extra lands onto the battlefield are all forms of ramp. Ramping lets you cast expensive spells sooner.
